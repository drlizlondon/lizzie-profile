How I think

Curiosity, judgement and the confidence to make complexity useful.

I have always been interested in why things work, where they fail and how they could work better. Emergency Medicine made that instinct practical. It taught me to understand a complicated system quickly, separate signal from noise and make thoughtful decisions when certainty is impossible.

Medicine and strategy ask the same essential questions: what is really happening, what matters most and what should we do next? Today I use that approach beyond the clinical setting. I challenge assumptions, uncover the real problem and turn ideas into practical strategies, products and services that survive contact with real life. The best tools help me think deeply, move quickly and test ideas against reality. Judgement still leads.
